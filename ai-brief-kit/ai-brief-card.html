The Language of AI

Talk about AI honestly, talk to it clearly, and know where to draw the line.

1 · Talking about AI: the 4-sentence brief

1
What it does
This [tool] takes [input] and gives [output], so [who] can [spend time on what].
Name the task it takes off someone’s desk, not the technology.
2
The honest promise
It [does what well], but it can [go wrong how], so [what we do about it].
Say how often it goes wrong, and what you do about it.
3
Who holds the wheel
For [type of task], [named role] [checks / decides] before [action].
Name a role, such as “the shift supervisor”, not “a human”.
4
The ask
I need [decision] from [who] by [date].
One decision they can say yes or no to. Not “thoughts?”

For sentence 2: what you can honestly promise

If it…You can promise
Follows fixed rules“It does exactly what we set, every time, and nothing else.”
Predicts“It gives a likelihood, not a certainty. Here’s how often it misses.”
Drafts or answers“It’s fast and usually right (here’s how often), but it can be wrong and sound sure. A person checks what matters.”
Acts on its own“It acts only within limits we set in advance, and a named person reviews what it does.”

For sentence 3: levels of control

LevelUse it for
1 · A person decidesSafety, big money, people’s rights, anything you can’t undo
2 · A person checksPolicy, customers, anything that leaves the team
3 · It runs aloneLow stakes, easy to spot, easy to undo
The test: if it’s wrong, who gets hurt, and can we undo it?

Same brief, different room: keep all four, change the order

RoomOrderStart with

2 · Talking to AI: the 5-part prompt

PartWhat it coversExample
1 · RoleWho the AI should act as“Act as a communications advisor.”
2 · TaskWhat you want done“Rewrite this project update as a 4-sentence brief.”
3 · ContextThe background, and who it’s for“It’s for a non-technical director who must approve a pilot.”
4 · FormatLength, style and structure“Plain words, under 100 words.”
5 · CheckWhat it should flag for you“Then list anything you assumed or aren’t sure about.”
A prompt you can copy (no confidential details)Act as a communications advisor. Rewrite the project below as a 4-sentence brief for a non-technical director: (1) what it does, in plain words; (2) what it does well, how often it goes wrong, and what we do about it; (3) who checks or decides before anything important happens; (4) the one decision I need, from whom, by when. Plain words, under 100 words. Then list anything you assumed or aren’t sure about. Project: [2–3 lines, no confidential details]

Before you use anything AI wrote

  • Is every fact true? It can be wrong and sound sure.
  • Did it add anything you didn’t give it? Numbers, names and promises are the usual suspects.
  • Would you sign your name to it? If not, it isn’t finished.

3 · Where to draw the line: seven questions before you use or buy an AI tool

FairnessDoes it work equally well for everyone it affects?
AccountabilityWho answers for it when it’s wrong?
TransparencyDo people know AI is involved, and can we explain its answers?
EthicsShould we do this at all, even if we can?
SafetyWhat’s the worst that happens if it fails, and who notices?
SecurityWhere does our data go, and who can see it?
SovereigntyWhere is our data stored and processed, and under whose laws?

Security: attackers use AI too

  • Never paste confidential information into an AI tool.
  • Use the AI tools your organization has approved.
  • Treat urgent messages, links and voice notes with extra suspicion.

Sustainability: use AI where it earns its place

  • Use it where it saves real effort.
  • Ask once, well, instead of five times.
  • Keep your own judgment for what matters most.
